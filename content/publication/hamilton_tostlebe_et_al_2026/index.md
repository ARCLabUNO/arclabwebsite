# Abstract
abstract = "This Phase 1 report examines how Iowa Department of Corrections staff and Iowa Board of Parole members assess release readiness and make release recommendations and decisions. Drawing on interviews and focus groups with 27 practitioners, the study finds that release decision-making is best understood as a process of “informed release” rather than a single assessment or checklist. Practitioners evaluate patterns of behavioral change over time—including institutional behavior, treatment engagement, accountability, and release planning—while also weighing information that may be incomplete, inconsistent, or unreliable. The findings also highlight differences in how IDOC and IBOP evaluate readiness, reflecting their distinct roles and access to information, as well as broader constraints such as prison overcrowding, limited housing and treatment resources, and public scrutiny. Existing risk assessments were viewed as useful but limited sources of information, particularly in their ability to capture behavioral change and current circumstances. The findings will inform the next phase of the project: developing and evaluating a parole decision-making tool tailored to Iowa that incorporates both static and dynamic information and supports more consistent, evidence-informed release decisions while maintaining public safety."
