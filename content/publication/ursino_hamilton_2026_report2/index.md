# Abstract
abstract = "This report examines the predictive performance of Arizona’s Offender Screening Tool (OST) and Field Reassessment Offender Screening Tool (FROST) and the development of locally weighted risk assessment scores for general and violent reconviction. Conducted as part of the Bureau of Justice Assistance-funded Adult Treatment Court Training Initiative, the study sought to improve risk assessment practices for individuals with histories of violent offending who may be eligible for treatment courts. Initial validation of 178,675 OST and 95,563 FROST assessments revealed weak or negligible predictive accuracy across most reconviction outcomes and substantial overprediction of violent reconviction risk. Using existing assessment items and Arizona-specific administrative data, researchers developed separate General and Violent Reconviction scores that demonstrated strong predictive accuracy and improved calibration. The two scores identified distinct risk factors, with violent reconviction most strongly associated with domestic violence status, prior violence-related convictions, and relationship problems, while general reconviction was driven primarily by current felony offenses, prior convictions, and recent drug use. Predictive performance was broadly consistent across sex and racial subgroups. Findings demonstrate that locally weighted risk assessments can substantially improve prediction of general and violent reconviction while leveraging existing assessment infrastructure. The resulting tools offer Arizona probation agencies and treatment courts more accurate, individualized information to inform supervision, treatment planning, and eligibility decisions."
