# Abstract
# abstract = "This report examines the predictive performance of South Dakota’s Level of Service Inventory-Revised (LSI-R) and Violence Risk Appraisal Guide (VRAG) and the development of locally weighted risk assessment scores for general and violent reconviction. Conducted as part of the Bureau of Justice Assistance-funded Adult Treatment Court Training Initiative, the study sought to improve risk assessment practices for individuals with histories of violent offending who may be eligible for treatment courts. Initial validation of assessments completed between March 2021 and March 2025 revealed negligible predictive accuracy for violent reconviction using the VRAG, weak-to-moderate accuracy using the LSI-R, and substantial overprediction of violent reconviction risk by both instruments. Using existing LSI-R items and South Dakota-specific assessment and administrative data, researchers developed separate General and Violent Reconviction scores that demonstrated moderate and strong predictive accuracy, respectively, and substantially improved calibration. The two scores identified distinct risk factors, with violent reconviction most strongly associated with current offenses, relationship dissatisfaction, residential instability, and substance use, while general reconviction was driven primarily by prior convictions, supervision violations, substance use, and attitudes toward supervision. Predictive performance was generally consistent across racial subgroups, although the Violent Reconviction score demonstrated stronger accuracy for White individuals than Non-White individuals. Findings demonstrate that locally weighted risk assessments can improve prediction of general and violent reconviction without requiring additional assessment items or changes to existing data collection procedures. The resulting tools offer South Dakota Court Services and treatment courts more accurate, individualized information to inform supervision, treatment planning, and eligibility decisions."
