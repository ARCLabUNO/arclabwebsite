# Abstract
 abstract = "As treatment courts increasingly consider individuals with violent offense histories for admission, accurately assessing violent recidivism risk has become critical to informing eligibility, supervision, and treatment decisions. However, commonly used correctional risk assessments were developed primarily to predict general recidivism rather than violence specifically. This report evaluates the predictive performance of two widely used instruments—the Level of Service Inventory-Revised (LSI-R) and Ohio Risk Assessment System (ORAS)—and presents newly developed violence risk scores using existing assessment information. Analyses draw on 151,204 LSI-R assessments from five states and 82,465 ORAS assessments from three states, with violent reconviction measured within three years of assessment. Findings indicate that the instruments' existing total scores demonstrated limited predictive accuracy in pooled samples (AUCs = 0.58 and 0.53, respectively) and systematically overpredicted violent reconviction. Reweighting assessment items substantially improved predictive performance, with the resulting LSI-R and ORAS Violent Reconviction scores achieving moderate accuracy (AUCs = 0.65 and 0.68) and improved calibration in held-out test samples. Additionally, localized assessments developed for Arizona, Alaska, and South Dakota demonstrated stronger predictive accuracy (AUCs = 0.71–0.77), underscoring the value of jurisdiction-specific assessment development. Together, these findings demonstrate that treatment courts can improve violent recidivism risk assessment using information already collected through existing assessment procedures, while emphasizing the importance of local validation, recalibration, and appropriate classification thresholds."
